{\Large\bfseries\color{Ink} Searching Binary Embeddings}\par
{\large Methods, Memory, Query Time, and Recall}\par
{\small 11 September 2026}\par\medskip
\begin{abstract}
This study compares three search methods for binary document embeddings: scanning selected
clusters, searching an inverted bitplane index, and looking up short bit patterns in order
of weighted mismatch cost. All methods use the same floating-point query and binary-document
score. The analysis accounts for stored index memory, temporary query memory, and the work
performed before and during document scoring. Recall estimates are derived under explicit
assumptions and compared with experimental evidence. The real-embedding study does not establish
a speed advantage for either alternative over the tuned scan. Controlled inputs show a small
key-lookup advantage when important coordinates remain fixed and a large bitplane advantage
when they vary by query. Larger-scale calculations are conditional forecasts rather than
measurements of a billion-document index. Worked examples connect the mathematical terms to
the operations implemented by each method.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} vector search; binary quantization; indexing; recall; memory.
