{\Large\bfseries Searching Binary Embeddings}\par
{\large Methods, Memory, Query Time, and Recall}\par
{\small Detailed study, 11 September 2026}\par\medskip
\begin{abstract}
This study compares cluster-based full scanning, inverted bitplane search, and weighted
key lookup for binary document embeddings. The methods share a floating-point query,
a binary-document score, and a common reference ranking. The analysis derives their
query-time work, index storage, temporary memory requirements, and recall under stated
probability models. Small examples explain the data structures, stopping rules, and
assumptions before the general calculations. Experiments include independent real queries,
controlled query-weight distributions, checks of predicted operation counts, and tests of
complete query-time estimates at larger collection sizes. Neither alternative establishes
a speed advantage over the tuned scan on the evaluated real embeddings. Controlled inputs
identify conditions favoring each alternative while exposing overlap between direct routing
and key lookup. Estimates at a billion documents remain conditional on the workload,
memory layout, and measured cost model. Failed approximations and unsuccessful settings are
retained to define the limits of the conclusions.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} vector search; binary quantization; indexing; recall; memory.
\medskip
The shared representation and scoring choices follow Exa's published vector-database
description~\cite{exa}. This report evaluates local implementations; it does not measure
Exa's production system. The shorter formal companion presents the central methods and results.
